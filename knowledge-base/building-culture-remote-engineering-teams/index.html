Building Culture in Remote Engineering Teams

Remote work isn't going anywhere. Here's how we've helped teams build strong engineering cultures across time zones, continents, and kitchen tables.

When the world went remote in 2020, many thought it would be temporary. Four years later, remote work has become the norm for engineering teams. But building culture remotely? That’s still a challenge many teams struggle with.

Over the past few years, we’ve helped dozens of engineering teams build thriving remote cultures. Here’s what actually works.

The Culture Challenge

Remote work solves many problems but creates new ones:

  • Isolation: Engineers feeling disconnected from their team
  • Communication gaps: Important context lost in translation
  • Timezone friction: Half the team is always asleep
  • Onboarding struggles: New hires taking months to feel integrated

The traditional solution—more meetings—often makes things worse.

Our Framework: Intentional Async Culture

We’ve developed a framework we call “Intentional Async Culture” that addresses these challenges systematically.

1. Documentation as Culture

Great documentation isn’t just about code—it’s about capturing the “why” behind decisions.

# Architecture Decision Record: API Rate Limiting

## Status
Accepted

## Context
We're experiencing API abuse from a small number of clients, 
affecting performance for legitimate users.

## Decision
Implement token bucket rate limiting at the API gateway level.

## Consequences
- Good: Protects service stability
- Good: Fair resource allocation
- Bad: Adds complexity to client implementations
- Bad: Requires careful tuning to avoid false positives

## Why This Matters to Our Team
This aligns with our core value of "user trust first"—we protect
the experience of our legitimate users even if it means more 
engineering complexity.

2. Rituals That Scale

Traditional team rituals need reimagining for remote teams:

Demo Fridays → Async Show & Tell

  • Engineers record 5-minute demos
  • Team watches and comments async
  • Monthly live session for Q&A

Coffee Chats → Donut Pairings

  • Automated random 1:1 pairings
  • Optional, but 85% participation
  • Mix across teams and levels

Retrospectives → Continuous Improvement

  • Weekly async retro board
  • Monthly synthesis meeting
  • Quarterly action review

3. Communication Architecture

We help teams design their communication architecture as carefully as their system architecture:

Communication Channels:
  Immediate (< 1 hour response):
    - Production incidents: PagerDuty
    - Blocked work: Slack #help-engineering
    
  Quick (< 4 hours):
    - Code reviews: GitHub notifications
    - Team questions: Slack team channels
    
  Async (< 24 hours):
    - Project updates: Notion
    - Decisions: GitHub Discussions
    - Knowledge sharing: Internal blog

Real Results from Real Teams

FinTech Startup (50 engineers, 12 timezones)

  • Reduced meeting time by 60%
  • Improved onboarding time from 3 months to 3 weeks
  • 94% report feeling “connected to team”

E-commerce Platform (200 engineers, fully remote)

  • Increased deployment frequency by 3x
  • Reduced “communication overhead” complaints by 80%
  • Voluntary attrition dropped to 5% annually

The Tools That Matter

After working with dozens of teams, here’s our recommended stack:

Essential:

  • Slack or Discord for chat
  • Notion or Confluence for docs
  • Loom or CleanShot for async video
  • Donut or similar for social connections

Nice to Have:

  • Gather or SpatialChat for virtual offices
  • Range or Geekbot for check-ins
  • Miro or FigJam for collaboration

Avoid:

  • Too many tools (pick one per category)
  • Surveillance software (trust your team)
  • Mandatory video policies (Zoom fatigue is real)

Mistakes We See (And How to Avoid Them)

1. Copying In-Office Culture

The biggest mistake? Trying to recreate the office online. Eight-hour Zoom rooms aren’t the answer. Instead, design new rituals that leverage async communication’s strengths.

2. Information Silos

When everyone’s remote, information silos form quickly. Combat this with:

  • Public by default documentation
  • Cross-team demos
  • Rotation programs

3. Ignoring Timezones

“Let’s just find a time that works for everyone” doesn’t scale. Instead:

  • Record important meetings
  • Rotate meeting times
  • Create timezone-aware teams

The Secret Sauce: Psychological Safety

The foundation of remote culture is psychological safety. Teams need to feel safe to:

  • Admit mistakes publicly
  • Ask “dumb” questions
  • Share works-in-progress
  • Disconnect when needed

We measure this quarterly with anonymous surveys and adjust our practices based on feedback.

Looking Forward

Remote engineering culture isn’t about recreating the office online—it’s about building something better. When done right, remote teams can be more inclusive, more productive, and yes, more connected than traditional teams.

The key is being intentional. Every communication, every ritual, every tool choice should serve your culture goals.
